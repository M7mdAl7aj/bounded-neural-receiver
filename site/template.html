A bounded neural receiver with exact classical fallback

A trained network improves a 5G-style receiver, but every bit it touches is held within a fixed distance, \(\Delta_{\max}\), of the classical receiver's answer. Set the bound to zero and the classical receiver comes back, bit for bit.

What the bound means for one bit

Set a classical LLR and a network LLR for one coded bit; the bound chosen with the slider above decides how far the output may move. An LLR \(L\) means \(P(b=1) = 1/(1+e^{-L})\): positive favours a 1, negative a 0, and the size is the confidence.

Other ways to combine the two receivers

The bound is one way to mix the classical and network LLRs. The paper also measured four generic rules on the same stored LLRs. Pick a rule and a setting and compare it with the bound chosen above. Bars show the gain over the classical receiver at the 10% point, in dB of \(E_{\mathrm{b}}/N_0\) (higher is better); whiskers span the three seeds.

\[L = \lambda\, L_{\mathrm{R3}} + (1-\lambda)\, L_{\mathrm{c}}\]

Blend: a fixed mix of the two LLRs on every bit. No cap: a fraction \(\lambda\) of any network excursion reaches the decoder.

\[L = \begin{cases} L_{\mathrm{R3}} & \text{if } \lvert L_{\mathrm{R3}} \rvert \ge \tau \\ L_{\mathrm{c}} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]

Confidence pick: per bit, use the network only where it is confident. \(\tau\) is set at a percentile of \(\lvert L_{\mathrm{R3}} \rvert\) measured in the training condition at each SNR.

\[\text{block LLRs} = \begin{cases} L_{\mathrm{R3}} & \text{if } \overline{\lvert L_{\mathrm{R3}} - L_{\mathrm{c}} \rvert} \le g \\ L_{\mathrm{c}} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]

Per-block switch: take the whole block from the network only where it agrees with the classical receiver on average. \(g\) is set at a percentile of that block statistic in the training condition.

\[L = \mathrm{clip}\left(L_{\mathrm{R3}},\ \pm C\right)\]

Plain clip: a fixed range for the network's LLRs, not anchored to the classical receiver. It limits confidence, not the deviation from the classical answer.

How the bound works

Nothing is retrained. The bound is applied to the two receivers' outputs, one bit at a time, just before the decoder.

1

Classical anchor

A standard receiver with IQ-imbalance compensation produces its log-likelihood ratio \(L_{\mathrm{c}}\) for every coded bit.

2

Bounded correction

The trained network proposes \(L_{\mathrm{R3}}\). The difference \(L_{\mathrm{R3}} - L_{\mathrm{c}}\) is clipped to \(\pm\Delta_{\max}\), so no bit can move further than that: \(\lvert L_{\mathrm{R5}} - L_{\mathrm{c}} \rvert \le \Delta_{\max}\).

3

Exact fallback

At \(\Delta_{\max} = 0\) the output is the classical receiver, bit for bit, whatever the network's weights. As \(\Delta_{\max} \to \infty\) it is the network alone.

Received slot→Classical receiver+Neural receiver→Clip to \(L_{\mathrm{c}} \pm \Delta_{\max}\)→LDPC decoder
